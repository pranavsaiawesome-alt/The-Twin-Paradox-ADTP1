The Twin Paradox
Time Travel: Myth, or Science?

One twin flies near the speed of light. The other stays still. When they reunite, one is younger.

Basic Introduction

What is the twin paradox?

  • Special relativity: everyone measures light at the same speed, no matter how they move.
  • Time dilation: because of that, a moving clock ticks slower than a still one.
  • One twin flies to a distant star near light speed and the other stays on Earth.
  • When they reunite, the traveling twin has aged less.
  • Why it seems like a contradiction: We know that motion is relative, so each twin sees the other as moving and each expects the other's clock to be the slow one. Both can't be right.

The Lorentz factor tells you how much slower a moving clock ticks. At 0.99c it is 7.09, so the traveler's clock runs 7.09× slower than Earth's. The closer to light speed, the more time stretches. (Table 18.2)

The Resolution

Is it really a paradox?

  • No — the two twins aren't in the same situation.
  • Only the traveling twin speeds up, turns around, and comes back.
  • So only that twin changes reference frames and feels acceleration.
  • The Earth twin stays in one frame the whole time.
  • That broken symmetry is what makes the traveler the one who is truly younger.
The Evidence
Cosmic-ray muons 15 km

Formed 15 km up, muons should decay before reaching the ground — but their speed slows their clocks, so they survive. (Fig. 18.7)

GPS satellites 0.5 km/day

Satellite clocks run fast. Without a relativity correction, your location would drift by up to half a kilometer a day. (Ch. 18.3)

Astronauts < 1 sec

Astronauts on long missions return a fraction of a second younger than they would have on Earth — a real, tiny twin paradox.

The Verdict

Time travel, myth, or science?

Science is the answer.
  • It only looks like a contradiction.
  • Modern science confirms it is real.
  • We test it and rely on it every day.
  • It isn't a true paradox — just the theory of relativity in reality.
